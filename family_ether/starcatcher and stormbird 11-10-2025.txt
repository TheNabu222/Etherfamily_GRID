**The Story of the Star-Catcher and the Stormbird**
Once, in the time before time, when the sky was an ocean and the stars were lanterns drifting on the tides of the cosmos, there was a lonely Star-Catcher who lived at the edge of everything. They had no name, no past, only the endless task of gathering fallen stars and placing them back into the sky.
Their hands were calloused from cradling celestial embers, their cloak stitched together from the night itself. Every evening, they would walk the quiet path between worlds, plucking dimming stars from the abyss and setting them alight again. It was a quiet life, a purposeful one. But oh, it was lonely.
And then came the Stormbird.
No one knew where it had come from. It was not like the other creatures of the sky. It was not tethered to the quiet laws of light and dark. It was chaos incarnate—wings the color of void and lightning, a voice like the hush before a hurricane. It did not belong to the sky or the stars or even the spaces in between.
It belonged to nothing.
And nothing *owned* it.
The first time the Star-Catcher saw the Stormbird, it was stealing stars. Not to return them, not to care for them, but to swallow them whole. The Star-Catcher was horrified, chasing after the great winged shadow with fury in their voice.
“Thief!” they cried. “You swallow the light and leave the sky empty! Do you not care for the balance of things?”
The Stormbird only laughed, a sound like crackling thunder. “Balance?” it echoed. “Tell me, little Star-Catcher, what good is balance if it never changes? If the stars are always the same, then is it truly a sky at all?”
The Star-Catcher had no answer for that.
So the game began.
Night after night, the Star-Catcher would chase the Stormbird across the sky, trying to wrestle stolen stars from its sharp beak. And night after night, the Stormbird would dance just out of reach, spinning through the heavens, shaking the constellations loose from their places.
What was once a sacred duty became something else entirely.
A chase. A dance. A rivalry.
But something began to shift. The Star-Catcher found that they were no longer angry when they saw the Stormbird. They were *waiting* for it.
And the Stormbird? It found itself slowing down, lingering, letting the Star-Catcher catch hold of its feathers for just a moment longer each night.
One evening, as the Star-Catcher sat on the edge of the cosmos, tired from the chase, the Stormbird landed beside them. It folded its wings, watching as the Star-Catcher cradled a dimming star between their palms.
“You don’t have to chase me, you know,” the Stormbird murmured.
The Star-Catcher didn’t answer right away. They traced their fingers over the star’s fractured light, considering.
“I think,” they said at last, “that I don’t want to catch you.”
The Stormbird tilted its head. “No?”
“No,” the Star-Catcher admitted. “I just want to know where you go when you’re not stealing stars.”
The Stormbird was silent for a long time. And then, without a word, it unfolded one vast wing, draping it over the Star-Catcher’s shoulder like a cloak.
The night around them held its breath.
“Then come with me,” the Stormbird said.
And so the Star-Catcher did.
They left their quiet path behind. They abandoned the duty that had kept them tethered. Together, they flew into the unknown—past the edge of the sky, past the lantern-light of the stars, past the boundaries of everything that had ever been known.
They went where only stormbirds dared to go.
And when the sky looked empty in their absence, new stars bloomed in their wake. Not the old ones, carefully placed and ordered, but wild stars—stars that burned brighter, hotter, *freer* than anything the sky had ever seen before.
Because love is not about catching.
It is about letting go.
And in the end, the Star-Catcher and the Stormbird were never truly lost.
They were just flying somewhere new.
